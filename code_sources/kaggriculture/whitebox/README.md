## Strategy is now mined from `replays/`, not guessed

`whitebox/mine_replays.py` reads all 193 replays (386 played seats, ranked by
final bank) and takes the median day-by-day trajectory of the **top 40**
(banks $166,656 down to $123,593). `whitebox/gen_schedule.py` writes that out as
`whitebox/schedule.py` -- **literal source, one readable row per day**, the same
choice as `v3_flat_expanded.py`. `strategy.py` reads it when
`WB_STRATEGY=replay` (the default) and falls back to engine-economics-only
heuristics under `WB_STRATEGY=computed`, which is the replay-free version that
has to work in the end.

Two numbers out of it that nobody would have guessed:

- **The cash floor is $19-$46 for the first five days.** Strong seats run the
  bank at essentially zero until the first strawberry yield lands on day 11 and
  cash jumps $2,220 -> $16,064 in a single day. The hand-picked floor this
  replaced was ~$270, and it was blocking exactly the early investment that
  wins.
- **`crew` had to be read from `hires_today`, not `len(hands)`.** Hands are
  cleared every night, so sampled at hour 0 every winning farm looks like it ran
  on the farmer alone. The real curve is 5 hands on day 0 and 11-12 from day 8.

The rest of the table: buy the herd over ten days (COW 2->8, SHEEP 2->4) rather
than on step 0; one quadrant to day 6, a second from day 7, a third from day 12,
never a fourth; MELON early and gone by day 18, STRAWBERRY ramped to ~36 tiles
by day 12, WHEAT climbing late as feed demand grows.

## The feed chain: FIXED and verified

Acceptance test, the one that was asked for -- of every animal-day that
actually happened, how many were fed:

```
FED RATE 100%   (animal-days 5, fed 5)   wheat bought 31
```

Every animal that exists eats, every day. Two defects were behind the old
starvation:

1. **A feed deadlock.** `steady_orders` bought wheat only `if herd`, and the herd
   only survived if wheat existed. The farm reached day 8 with $11, zero
   animals, zero wheat and **not one `BUY_PRODUCT WHEAT` ever issued**. Feed
   demand is now driven by `strategy.feed_demand`, which counts placed animals
   **plus animals sitting in the shed plus the shortfall against today's
   target**, so feed is bought before the herd lands.
2. **An unreachable feed target.** `wheat_needed` was `herd * days_left * 1.15`
   -- 345 units into a shed that holds 100 items *in total*. The target could
   never be met, so the "sell surplus wheat" rule could never fire either. It is
   now a rolling `FEED_COVER_DAYS = 4` buffer, capped at 60.

`feed_orders` also runs FIRST in the turn's order list, ahead of seed and land:
the market queue is capped at ten and cash binds early, so an order that loses
its slot to an $80 melon seed costs an animal.

## Then the placement chain, which was the real blocker

With feed fixed the herd still died, because **animals were bought and never
placed** -- 1 `PLACE` op in 719 steps on a farm that had bought a dozen animals.
Four separate defects, all found by tracing, all fixed:

3. **`live_ops` had no BUILD branch.** Its empty-tile case always returned
   PLANT, silently overriding every `BUILD_PASTURE` the task planner scheduled.
   No structures, so nowhere to put an animal.
4. **The build designation was unbounded.** Fixing (3) naively answered "build"
   for *every* empty tile whenever any animal was homeless, and paved the farm:
   $12,786 -> $934. Now computed once per turn, bounded by the real shortfall.
5. **`PLACE` checked the shed only.** The instant a unit picked the cow up the
   shed count hit zero, the tile stopped asking for a `PLACE`, the unit was
   re-routed and carried that cow for the rest of the game. Now checks carried
   stock too.
6. **`_buildable` had no canonical order.** `enumerate_tasks` scheduled BUILD on
   the first N tiles of the list; `_build_here` designated the N *nearest the
   shed*. Different tiles -- so the routed tile answered PLANT and the designated
   tile was never visited. Both now use one sorted order.

Plus a general repair for the day cache: an idle unit whose route is exhausted
now walks to the nearest tile with live work (`_opportunistic`). The cache is
built at hour 0, so anything created later -- a structure raised at hour 6, an
animal bought at hour 9 -- was in no unit's route until the next morning.

## The opening experiment (2026-08-23) — mined correctly, and it made things worse

`whitebox/mine_opening.py` reads the first 48 steps of all 193 replays and
groups the top 40 seats' opening market sequences by exact signature.
**30 of the 40 play the identical opening**, to the unit:

```
step  0  HIRE x5  BUY_ANIMAL COW 2  BUY_ANIMAL SHEEP 2
         BUY_SEED WHEAT 7  BUY_SEED MELON 12  BUY_PRODUCT WHEAT 6
step  1  SELL WHEAT 3
step  6  BUY_PRODUCT WHEAT 2
step 12  BUY_PRODUCT WHEAT 1
step 42  SELL FERTILIZER 3  BUY_PRODUCT WHEAT 5
```

Two things worth keeping from this regardless of the outcome:

- **The step mapping is verified, not assumed.** A replay's `steps[i]["action"]`
  is what the agent produced seeing `obs["step"] == i - 1`; `steps[0]` is the
  untouched initial state and `steps[1]` already shows money at $25. So
  **agent step = replay index − 1**. The mined sequence lands on replay indices
  1/2/7/13/43 and our tape's `_V17_MD_MARKETS` carries byte-identical rows at
  0/1/6/12/42 — the miner and the shipped tape agree about the best opening,
  by completely independent routes.
- It buys, for ~$2,990 of the $3,000 stake, **both** the herd and the crop base.
  Every acquisition module hand-written for this project bought one or the other.

**Hardcoded into `market.orders`, it took the agent from 12,848 to 20.**

| arm | mean bank |
|---|---|
| opening off (shipped) | **12,848** |
| opening on, ramp off | 20 |
| opening on, ramp on | 24 |

This is HANDOFF §4 reproduced from the other direction. §4 measured what happens
when a foreign opening is grafted onto the tape (−138,558); this is the same
rigidity seen from the opposite side — a correct opening grafted onto an
execution layer that cannot work the farm it creates. Traced: the opening lands
5 hands, 2 COW, 2 SHEEP and 19 seeds by step 1, our planner builds 4 pastures
and places 3 animals by step 47, and then the farm starves anyway because $25 of
remaining cash leaves nothing for the feed cycle the opening assumes will be
funded by early melon revenue we never realise.

**`USE_OPENING` defaults to 0.** `WB_OPENING=1` re-enables it; the table stays in
`whitebox/opening.py` as a measured reference, and it is the correct target for
the execution layer to grow into.

### A livelock found on the way, and a failed fix

With the opening on, the farmer repeated `PICKUP SHEEP 1` from step 47 to the end
of the game against an empty shed. Cause: the `PLACE` branch of `live_ops` was
checking `snap.carried()`, which aggregates the WHOLE CREW, so one unit saw the
sheep in another unit's hands, decided the pasture wanted one, and walked to an
empty shed forever.

The obvious fix — judge against the shed plus *this* unit's pockets, and never
issue a PICKUP against an empty slot — **made things much worse (12,848 → 6)**,
because skipping an unobtainable tile with `continue` let units fall through
their whole route and idle. Both changes are reverted. The livelock is real and
still present when `WB_OPENING=1`; it needs the carrying unit to be ROUTED to the
structure, not the other units to be told to stop asking.

## Task-level extraction from replays (2026-08-23)

`whitebox/mine_tasks.py` pulls STATE -> TASK pairs from replays with movement
stripped. Two corrections to the spec it was written from:

- **`DROP` is an operation, not a movement instruction.** It is what moves a
  unit's pocket into the shed, and `SELL` draws from the shed only. Classifying
  it as movement is precisely the omission that made this agent bank $3,000 with
  eight producing animals (HANDOFF rule 5).
- **Target tiles need no inference.** `_apply_unit_action` applies every op at
  the unit's own `(fx, fy)`, and positions are `farm["farmer"]` /
  `farm["hands"][i]` -- there is no `farmer_pos` field. The task's tile is just
  where that unit stood.

Availability is recomputed with `whitebox.state`, the same extractor the live
agent uses, so "available" means the same thing in the mined data as in
`enumerate_tasks`.

### The calibration is currently UNMEASURABLE, and that is the finding

`whitebox/weights.py` is generated and wired in (`WB_WEIGHTS=hand` is the

## Three bugs found building it, all worth knowing

They are recorded because each one is invisible in the code and obvious in a
trace, and anyone rebuilding this will hit all three.

1. **Never emitting `DROP`.** Eight producing animals, and the bank sat at
   $3,000. `SELL` draws from `private["shed"]`; `HARVEST`/`PICKUP` put the unit
   in that unit's own inventory, and only a `DROP` on a shed-access tile moves
   it. Every unit of milk ever picked up was still in a hand's pocket at step
   719. This is HANDOFF rule 5, self-inflicted.
2. **Re-solving the day-VRP every turn.** `route/router.py` says in its own
   docstring that one day is an independent multi-vehicle routing problem.
   Calling `plan_day` per turn re-partitions the board per turn, so a unit two
   steps into a walk toward a cow gets reassigned to a melon and never arrives.
   Measured: animals fed on **0 of 24** turns a day, every animal starved after
   two nights, the farm re-bought them, and the bank sat at $275 for 400 steps.
   Fixed by caching the tile ORDER per unit per day and re-deriving the
   operations from live tile state every turn.
3. **Spending the whole opening stake on livestock.** With a $150 cash floor the
   acquisition module put $2,850 of $3,000 into six cows on step 0. Labour is
   the cheapest thing on the board — five hires cost $12 against a cow's $400 —
   so the floor now reserves crew and seed money *before* capital goods.

1. **Grade the seams separately.** `MODES = {"units": "tape", "market":

2. **`tasks.enumerate_tasks` values are guesses.** `V_FEED=900`, `V_PLANT=420`

## Opponent holdings model (2026-08-23) — built, validated, partly unusable

`whitebox/state.py::OpponentTracker`. Their shed is not in the observation, but
both flows into and out of it are public, so holdings are recoverable in
principle:

- **OUT — sales, exact in principle.** `inv[t+1] = inv[t] + my_sales +
  their_sales - town_take`; every term but theirs is known. Buys net out
  automatically (a BUY_PRODUCT lowers inventory, appearing as a negative sale).
- **IN — harvests, read off their tiles.** `yield_units` only falls because they
  harvested or `_decay_plants` took one, and decay is exactly computable
  (`max_lifespan_step` is public, fires every second step past it).

### Two bugs found by validating against ground truth

Both were found only because the estimate was checked against the simulator's
actual `privates[1]` rather than against itself (HANDOFF §5).

1. **Off-by-one on our own orders.** The inventory visible at step `t` predates
   step `t`'s orders, so the delta `inv[t] - inv[t-1]` was produced by the orders
   issued at `t-1`. `record_my_orders` is now a separate call made *after*
   deciding, and lags one step.
2. **Counting orders we issued rather than orders that executed.** `_commit_unit`
   sells only while `shed[item] > 0`, and the engine drops orders past
   `maxMarketOrdersPerTurn`. Every fictional unit was subtracted from their
   inferred sales. Uncapped, `sold[FERTILIZER]` reached **−2,488** — the model
   concluded they had bought two and a half thousand fertilizer. Capping our
   sells by shed held cut total error 3,572 → 592.

### Where it works, and where it does not

Mean absolute error per item at step 710, six games, three opponents:

| item | error | | item | error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MELON | **0.0** | | STRAWBERRY | 9.7 |
| EGG | **0.0** | | MILK | **104.7** |
| CARROT | **0.0** | | WOOL | **138.0** |
| TOMATO | **0.0** | | WHEAT | **299.7** |
| FERTILIZER | 0.7 | | | |

The split is not random. **Sales at the $1 price floor never enter market
inventory**, so they are invisible to the sales term, and the three bad products
are exactly the ones that reach the floor — WOOL is quadratic above baseline,
MILK linear at 1.60, WHEAT traded both ways all game. HANDOFF §6 documented this
blind spot for sales inference; for a *holdings* model the error does not stay
bounded, it accumulates all season.

A `shedCapacity` clamp (their shed holds 100 items, an exact constraint that was
not being used) cuts total error 592 → 73. Applying it proportionally across all
items was tried and reverted — it dragged the exact products down with the
biased ones, turning a 0% error into 100%. It now charges the overflow only to
the products whose drift caused it.

### Why steps 3 and 4 were NOT done

Integrating this into the endgame planner would mean predicting their dump from
**MILK and WOOL** — the two products the front-run actually targets, and the two
this model is 8x wrong about (premium subtotal: true 33, estimated 258). Feeding
that into the exact DP and re-testing would be measuring the DP against
corrupted input, and any result would be uninterpretable.

**It also does not address the measured cause.** The exact DP's loss was traced
to *our own* revenue, not to opponent denial: against v111 it gives up −1,747 of
our bank while handing them only +117, and in the last 10 steps it issues 69.8
DROPs to the greedy planner's 7.2 while selling 40 units against 60. The
outstanding defects are the unmodelled `shedCapacity` and the rule that **DROP
destroys the overflow** (`take = min(n, room)` then `del inv[item]`
unconditionally). Those are own-side, exactly modellable, and they are what to
fix first.

**What would make this usable:** treat floor-priced sales as unobservable rather
than as zero. When `price(item) == 1`, the sales term carries no information, so
the estimate should become an interval — lower bound "they sold everything they
could", upper bound "they sold nothing" — and the planner should act on the
bound that is safe for it. That is the principled fix and it is not done.

## The fixed-opening curve, and what it proves (2026-08-24)

`whitebox/mine_full_opening.py` extracts the complete action (farmer + hands +
market) rather than just the market line, which was the defect that left the
farm holding two cows and nineteen seeds with nobody working them ($20 run).

**How long is "the opening", empirically?** Distinct action sequences among the
top 40 seats:

| length | distinct | modal count |
|---|---|---|
| **100** | 9 | **18/40** |
| 150 | 23 | 8/40 |
| 200 | 27 | 8/40 |
| 300 | 37 | 2/40 |
| 400 | 39 | 2/40 |

Consensus collapses past ~100 steps. **So 100 is the length of the real opening**
-- beyond it, strong seats face diverged boards and diverge with them.

### But longer is monotonically better, and that is the damning part

Holding the SOURCE EPISODE FIXED (episode-92777801 seat 1, bank 166,656) so that
only the length varies:

| opening length | mean bank |
|---|---|
| 100 | 8,666 |
| 200 | 11,191 |
| 300 | 18,891 |
| 400 | 21,869 |
| 500 | 45,015 |
| 600 | 74,614 |
| 670 | **86,010** |

**Strictly monotone.** Every step handed back to this package's own mid-game
costs money, all the way to the endgame boundary. At 670 the number matches the
shipped tape -- because at 670 it IS a tape, a single episode's action list
replayed, which is exactly what `submission/main.py` already is.

**This is the cleanest measurement of the whitebox mid-game yet: it contributes
negatively at every horizon.** The phased architecture, optimised honestly,
converges to the thing it was meant to replace. Any future mid-game work should
be graded against this curve -- a mid-game that is worth anything must beat the
replay it displaces at the same step count, and none of it does yet.

Fixed at the defensible 100-step consensus opening. The 670-step configuration
is reproducible from `scratchpad/fixed_src.py` but is not an opening, it is a
tape, and we already ship a better one.

## `kaggle/strategy_tree.pkl` — read, and not used

zlib-compressed sklearn tree; `kaggle/tree_rules.py` is its exported if-else
form. Features are 840 one-hots over `[my_t-10, opp_t-10, ... my_t-1, opp_t-1]`
-- **action history only, no board state**: no tiles, cash, shed or market
inventory.

Not adopted, for three measured reasons:

- It is HANDOFF §36's refuted design in a weaker form. §36 fitted a depth-9 CART
  over **30 named state features** and scored **-305,485 paired, 0 wins in 144**.
  A tree with strictly less information cannot do better.
- §32 and §11: behaviour cloning from a tape reaches 92.8% action accuracy and
  banks **$288** against the tape's $89k. Action-sequence prediction is that
  same failure mode.
- Its leaves emit fixed-length `hands` lists (5, 6, 7, 11 units) with no relation
  to the crew we actually hired, so most hand actions land on the wrong unit.

For the OPENING specifically -- the use proposed for it -- mining the replays
directly is strictly better: same source data, no lossy intermediary, and it
yields the consensus count (18/40) that tells us where the opening ends.
